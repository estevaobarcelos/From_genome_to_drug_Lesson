  PRACTICAL EXERCISES — Clustal Omega & EMBOSS Needle
  From Genome to Drug | Lesson II
  Università degli Studi di Perugia
==============================================================

These two exercises use the same protein — CTLA-4 — but
ask different biological questions that require different
alignment tools.

  EMBOSS Needle  → comparing TWO sequences, globally
  Clustal Omega  → comparing MULTIPLE sequences at once

--------------------------------------------------------------


==============================================================
  EXERCISE A — EMBOSS Needle
  Global pairwise alignment: Human vs Mouse CTLA-4
==============================================================

BACKGROUND
--------------------------------------------------------------
You want to develop a therapeutic antibody against CTLA-4.
Before testing it in a mouse model, you need to know:
how similar is mouse CTLA-4 to the human protein?
If they are too different, your antibody may not cross-react
and your mouse model may be invalid.

--------------------------------------------------------------
URL: www.ebi.ac.uk/Tools/psa/emboss_needle

QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. What is the % Identity between human and mouse CTLA-4?

Q2. What is the % Similarity?
    Why is similarity always >= identity?
    Hint: think about what BLOSUM62 counts as "similar".

Q3. How many gaps appear in the alignment?
    In which region of the protein do they occur —
    signal peptide, extracellular domain, or cytoplasmic tail?

Q4. Look at the alignment output carefully.
    Find the region corresponding to the Ig V-set domain
    (approximately residues 38–161 of the human sequence).
    Is this region more or less conserved than the rest?

Q5. Based on your result — would you expect an antibody
    raised against human CTLA-4 to cross-react with
    mouse CTLA-4?
    What does this mean for preclinical testing of ipilimumab?

Q6. Ipilimumab is a FULLY HUMANISED antibody.
    Researchers had to create a separate mouse-specific
    anti-CTLA-4 antibody (clone 9H10) for mouse experiments.
    Based on your alignment result, does this make sense?
    Explain why.

EXPECTED RESULTS (read after answering)
--------------------------------------------------------------
% Identity:   approximately 76%
% Similarity: approximately 83-85%
Gaps:         concentrated outside the Ig V-set domain
Conclusion:   antibody humanisation was essential —
              the sequences differ enough that human
              ipilimumab does not reliably cross-react
              with mouse CTLA-4


==============================================================
  EXERCISE B — Clustal Omega
  Multiple sequence alignment: CTLA-4 across five species
==============================================================

BACKGROUND
--------------------------------------------------------------
You want to identify which regions of CTLA-4 are conserved
across vertebrate evolution. Conserved positions are likely
functionally essential — mutating them is lethal.
This is exactly the analysis researchers used to identify
the Ig V-set domain as the critical functional region
and the target for ipilimumab.

QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. Look at the alignment output.
    The asterisk (*) marks perfectly conserved positions —
    same amino acid in ALL five species.
    Which region has the most asterisks?
    Signal peptide, Ig V-set domain, transmembrane,
    or cytoplasmic tail?

Q2. Download or view the phylogenetic tree
    (Results → Phylogenetic Tree tab).
    Which two species cluster closest together?
    Does this match what you know about evolution?

Q3. Find a column with an asterisk (*) in the Ig V-set domain.
    What amino acid is it?
    Why would this position be conserved across 300 million
    years of vertebrate evolution?

Q4. Now find a column with no conservation symbol —
    a column where all five species have different amino acids.
    In which part of the protein is it located?
    What does lack of conservation tell you about
    the functional importance of that position?

Q5. The chicken sequence is shorter than the mammalian ones.
    Gaps appear at the C-terminal end.
    What does this suggest about the evolution of the
    cytoplasmic tail across vertebrates?

Q6. Compare the Ig V-set domain across all five species.
    Is this domain more conserved than the signal peptide?
    Explain why in terms of natural selection.

Q7. Ipilimumab was designed to block the human Ig V-set domain.
    Based on your alignment — would you predict it to also
    block chicken CTLA-4?
    What regions of the sequence support your answer?

EXPECTED RESULTS (read after answering)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Most conserved:  Ig V-set domain — the CD80/CD86 binding site
Least conserved: signal peptide and cytoplasmic tail
Tree:            human and chimpanzee cluster together,
                 then mouse and rat, then chicken as outgroup
Chicken:         shorter C-terminus — cytoplasmic tail diverged
Conclusion:      functional constraint keeps the binding domain
                 conserved; structurally peripheral regions
                 tolerate more variation


==============================================================
  CONNECTING THE TWO EXERCISES
==============================================================

After completing both exercises, answer these final questions:

FINAL Q1.
EMBOSS Needle gave you a % identity between TWO species.
Clustal Omega showed you conservation across FIVE species.
Which tool gives you more information about which positions
are functionally essential? Why?

FINAL Q2.
You found that the Ig V-set domain is the most conserved
region in both exercises. This is where ipilimumab binds.
Is this a coincidence?
What is the relationship between evolutionary conservation
and drug target identification?

FINAL Q3.
A researcher proposes a new CTLA-4-blocking antibody
that targets the cytoplasmic tail instead of the Ig V-set.
Based on your alignment results — do you think this is
a good idea? What problem might arise?
